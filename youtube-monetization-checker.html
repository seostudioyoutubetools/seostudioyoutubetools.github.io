What a monetization checker can really tell you

Public clues are not private account records. Use them carefully—and check your own status at the source.

What is a YouTube monetization checker?

A YouTube monetization checker is a third-party tool that attempts to infer whether a channel or video may be monetized using signals available to it. A result is an estimate, not confirmation of a channel’s YouTube Partner Program (YPP) membership, earnings or payout.

Ads alone are not proof that the creator receives a share: YouTube may show ads on videos from channels that are not in YPP. A checker cannot see another creator’s private eligibility or account settings unless that information is explicitly available to it.

How to check your own channel

  1. Sign in to YouTube Studio for the channel you manage.
  2. Open the Earn section to see eligibility progress, available features and any application status.
  3. Read the current requirements and any account-specific notices before applying.

For someone else’s channel, use a third-party result only as a tentative clue. Do not treat an unknown result as proof that the channel is not monetized.

Understanding YouTube Partner Program eligibility

For the standard ad-revenue sharing route, YouTube’s published threshold includes 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 valid public watch hours in the previous 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the previous 90 days. Meeting a threshold does not guarantee acceptance: YouTube reviews the channel and other account and policy requirements apply.

Earlier access is different. In eligible regions, an expanded YPP tier can provide access to some fan-funding and Shopping features at lower thresholds. It is not the same as full ad-revenue eligibility. Availability and rules can change; verify them in YouTube Studio and YouTube Help.

Watch hours and Shorts views

The two routes use different rolling windows. Valid public watch hours generally come from eligible long-form public videos; watch time from views in the Shorts Feed does not count toward the 4,000-hour threshold. The Shorts route uses valid public Shorts views instead. Private, unlisted or deleted content and other exclusions may affect what counts; the Earn dashboard is the right place to check your progress.

Interpreting a third-party result

A checker may report “likely,” “unlikely” or “unknown.” Its methodology may be incomplete, public signals can change, and monetization can vary by feature or video. A channel may be in YPP without earning on every upload. Never use a checker to estimate someone’s income or make a business decision without confirmation.

GOOD TO KNOW

Frequently asked questions

Can I confirm another channel’s YPP status?

A public tool cannot reliably confirm private account status. Ask the channel owner if confirmation is important.

Does an ad mean the creator earns money?

Not necessarily. YouTube can place ads on content even when the channel is not eligible for a revenue share.

Do requirements ever change?

Yes. Features, thresholds, locations and policy requirements can change. Check YouTube’s current guidance and your Earn dashboard.
